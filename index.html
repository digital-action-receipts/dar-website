Why evidence matters

Consequential work can no longer live only inside a chat transcript or vendor log.

AI agents, employees, contractors, and service providers can research, draft, recommend, modify records, use tools, and prepare external actions. When the work affects money, rights, records, contracts, or public services, an authorized reviewer needs a durable account of both execution and restraint.

Authority becomes visible

Show what the operator was asked and permitted to do, who delegated that authority, and which limits applied.

Evidence becomes reviewable

Connect actions to approved sources, tools, systems, policies, approvals, results, and material exceptions.

Boundaries become provable

Record what was refused, blocked, escalated, or intentionally left unsubmitted when authority ran out.

How it works

Turn consequential actions into reviewable evidence.

DAR sits alongside existing agents and workflows. It records observable actions, authority, evidence, policy checks, and human decisions without pretending to capture hidden model reasoning or replacing the original system.

1

Record the requested action, permitted scope, prohibited actions, principal, and required approval boundaries.

2

3

Verify and reconstruct

Two sides of accountability

Proof of action. Proof of restraint.

An accountable workflow must explain not only what occurred, but also which consequential actions did not occur and why.

Proof of action

Evidence of the work that was actually performed.

  • Request and intended outcome
  • Actor, agent, operator, or vendor identity
  • Tools, systems, sources, and policy references
  • Approvals, material actions, results, and timestamps
  • Integrity hashes and verification data

Proof of restraint

Evidence that authority boundaries and required controls were respected.

  • Prohibited actions and approval gates
  • Refusals, blocks, exceptions, and escalations
  • Actions declined or left unsubmitted
  • Human review and retained decision authority
  • Unresolved evidence and explicit limitations
Trust must be earned

Integrity, identity, and outcome are different questions.

The current demonstration evaluates receipt structure, content integrity, signature validity, issuer recognition, key status, and event-time validity separately. A valid signature alone does not prove that the underlying action was authorized, accurate, successful, fair, or complete.

A focused assurance primitive

More than a log. More honest than a blanket “verified.”

A DAR separates what can be cryptographically verified from what still requires human, policy, or outcome review. Missing evidence and unresolved claims remain visible instead of being collapsed into a single pass indicator.

Priority use cases

A neutral layer across agents, vendors, supervisors, and buyers.

DAR is designed to complement existing workflow, procurement, governance, case-management, and service-delivery systems rather than replace them.

AI-agent accountability

Document assigned authority, evidence, tool activity, approvals, results, exceptions, and prohibited actions for consequential agent work.

Third-party vendor verification

Create evidence that agreed work occurred, approved systems and processes were used, required approvals were obtained, and expected deliverables were addressed—without invasive employee surveillance.

Human–AI approval chains

Show where AI assisted, where a person reviewed or corrected the work, and who retained final decision authority.

Government and regulated workflows

Support evidence-backed research, analysis, document preparation, and human approval while recording strict boundaries around submission, certification, signature, eligibility, and legal representation.

Dispute and audit support

Help an authorized reviewer reconstruct observable actions and evidence without claiming DAR decides liability, compliance, eligibility, or legal sufficiency.

Contracted-work evidence

Give buyers and service providers a shared, portable record of requested work, delivery steps, exceptions, acceptance, and unresolved obligations.

Clear boundaries

Evidence without surveillance or lock-in.

DAR is meant to make specific actions verifiable, not to become another monitoring platform or a copy of every system log.

What DAR is

  • An independent evidence and authorization layer
  • Portable assurance for consequential agent actions
  • A record of scope, evidence, controls, and human decisions
  • A way to reconstruct actions across tools and organizations

What DAR is not

  • The AI model or operational decision system

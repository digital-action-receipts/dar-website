How it works

Create evidence at the moment of action.

DAR adds a small, portable proof layer to existing systems. It records the minimum facts needed to verify an event later without copying private logs or coupling the verifier to the original platform.

1

Observe an action

A person, service, workflow, or agent performs a defined digital action.

2

Issue a receipt

The issuer records the action, time, parties, target, and evidence references in a signed, tamper-evident receipt.

3

Verify anywhere

A recipient checks integrity, issuer identity, key status, and supporting trust anchors independently.

Trust must be earned

A receipt chain is only the beginning.

DAR grades evidence by the independent trust anchors supporting it. The score communicates evidence strength without asking users to understand cryptography.

A focused evidence primitive

Small enough to travel. Strong enough to verify.

A DAR is a machine-verifiable record with a human-readable explanation. It captures what happened, when it happened, who issued the evidence, and how that evidence can be checked.

What a receipt can include

✓Action type and identifier
✓Actor and issuer identity
✓Event and issuance times
✓Target and context references
✓Evidence hashes
✓Signature and key identifier
✓Trust anchors
✓Independent verification result
Industry-neutral by design

One receipt model, many kinds of action.

DAR is infrastructure, not a vertical application. Systems can use the same evidence pattern wherever an action must be proven across organizational or technical boundaries.

ActionReceipt evidenceWhy portability matters
Approval or reviewWho reviewed what, when, and under which policyThe recipient can verify the decision without private workflow access
Publication or deliveryAsset fingerprint, destination, time, and issuerProof remains useful after the source platform changes
Automated executionAgent, instruction, tool, target, and result referenceEvidence can cross systems without sharing full logs
Consent or authorizationScope, parties, terms reference, and timeEach party can retain and verify the same record
Transfer or handoffSender, recipient, object, and acknowledgmentThe chain of evidence survives organizational boundaries
Clear boundaries

Evidence without surveillance or lock-in.

DAR is meant to make specific actions verifiable, not to become another monitoring platform or a copy of every system log.

What DAR is

  • A neutral receipt format for digital actions
  • Append-only, tamper-evident evidence
  • Portable across tools and organizations
  • Independently verifiable

What DAR is not

  • A monitoring or analytics platform
  • A replacement for internal logs
  • A claim that an action was correct or lawful
  • A product tied to one industry or buyer
